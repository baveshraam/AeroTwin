SLIDE 1 — PROBLEM STATEMENT
================================================================
Aircraft structural inspection is still manual, visual and calendar-driven —
and it fails in three ways at the point of work:

- Inconsistent detection. Probability of Detection for fine cracks and early
  corrosion varies with inspector skill, experience and fatigue; severity is
  judged against SRM limits by eye, so the airworthiness measurement is rarely
  repeatable.
- Poor traceability. Findings sit on paper task cards and siloed systems. There
  is no continuous condition history per tail number, so "did this crack grow
  since the last C-check?" is genuinely hard to answer.
- Reactive and costly. Inspection runs under AOG conditions (~ Rs 8 lakh-1.25
  crore per hour). Fixed-interval checks over-maintain healthy structure while
  failing to advance maintenance on fast-growing defects.

And cloud AI cannot help: ITAR-restricted hangars are network-isolated and
imagery legally cannot leave the site.

================================================================
SLIDE 2 — OBJECTIVE
================================================================
Deliver an offline, edge-resident inspection capability that runs on a
Raspberry Pi-class device and, in a single pass:

1. Detects, segments and severity-grades structural defects against SRM
   allowable-damage limits — turning a subjective visual judgement into a
   repeatable, millimetre-accurate measurement.
2. Records every finding in a persistent digital twin per tail number, tracks
   defect progression across checks, and predicts the flight cycle at which a
   defect crosses its limit.
3. Drafts the manual-cited work order via an agentic copilot, with the
   certifying engineer retaining final authority.

Goal: shift aircraft structural maintenance from reactive/calendar-based to
predictive/condition-based — fully offline and ITAR-compliant.

================================================================
SLIDE 3 — APPROACH
================================================================
Edge-first, because the domain forces it:
- Hangars are RF-restricted and often network-dead.
- ITAR/EAR/data-sovereignty: OEM and defence imagery cannot hit public cloud.
- AOG economics: a verdict in seconds at the aircraft beats an upload loop.

Build one narrow, flawless vertical slice; architect the rest:
- BUILT (L0-L3): edge detect + segment + mm severity grade -> per-tail twin ->
  Paris-law progression -> agentic, manual-cited work order.
- ROADMAP (L4-L6): federated fleet learning, closed-loop MRO ops, AR overlay.

Data-scarcity answer (built in, not bolted on): self-supervised backbones +
unsupervised anomaly detection + synthetic defects + active learning, so we do
not depend on thousands of labelled aerospace defect images.

================================================================
SLIDE 4 — SOLUTION OVERVIEW
================================================================
AeroTwin-Edge — Condition-Based Structural Health Management at the edge.

The unbroken chain (one tail, one defect, all offline on the Pi):
  Crack on a skin panel
    -> DETECT + GRADE  "ATA 53, stringer 14, crack 8.0 mm — MAJOR (67% of limit)"
    -> TWIN            "tail VT-XYZ, was 6.0 mm last C-check, +2.0 mm"
    -> PREDICT         "crosses the 12 mm SRM limit in ~40 flight cycles"
    -> AGENT           drafts a work order citing SRM 53-xx; certifying staff sign
  Hash-chain logged. Pi in airplane mode. Verdict in seconds.

Not "an AI that spots cracks" — the system that decides, per tail, what to fix
and when, and proves it to the regulator.

================================================================
SLIDE 5 — TECHNICAL IMPLEMENTATION
================================================================
IMPLEMENTED (this POC):
- Detection/segmentation: YOLO segmentation with SAHI tiling for thin, small,
  high-aspect-ratio cracks (pixel-accurate masks, not coarse boxes).
- Severity/sizing: OpenCV + ArUco fiducial -> pixels to millimetres, graded
  against a configurable SRM allowable-damage-limit table.
- Digital twin: per-tail store; findings pinned to ATA zone / station / stringer.
- Progression: Paris-law fatigue crack-growth model -> cycles-to-limit.
- Agentic copilot: retrieval-grounded drafting over an AMM/SRM excerpt corpus,
  citation-required, human sign-off gate.
- Edge runtime: offline execution on Raspberry Pi 5; latency shown on screen.

ARCHITECTED (roadmap):
- Unsupervised anomaly detection (PatchCore / EfficientAD) for unknown defects.
- INT8 quantization + pruning/distillation; optional Hailo-8L accelerator.
- Federated learning (Flower / FedAvg) with secure aggregation + differential
  privacy — models improve across the fleet; raw imagery never leaves the site.
- Hash-chained, tamper-evident audit trail; signed models; OTA registry.

Stack: PyTorch, Ultralytics/YOLO, OpenCV, ONNX Runtime / TFLite / NCNN,
FastAPI/Streamlit, SQLite, a small local LLM for RAG. Bootstrap data: NEU-DET,
SDNET2018, GAPs + synthetic defects.

================================================================
SLIDE 6 — CHALLENGES FACED
================================================================
- Data scarcity (no large aerospace defect corpus)
    -> unsupervised anomaly detection + synthetic generation + transfer + active
       learning, designed in from the start.
- Thin, tiny cracks evade naive detectors
    -> SAHI high-resolution tiling + a segmentation head for accurate geometry.
- Pixels are not airworthiness
    -> ArUco fiducial calibration to millimetres, with stated measurement
       uncertainty (gauge R&R mindset).
- Edge compute ceiling (Pi 5 has no built-in NPU)
    -> INT8 quantization, pruning, distillation; optional Hailo-8L; "minimum
       resource" is the thesis, not a compromise.
- LLM hallucination is unacceptable in a safety context
    -> citation-required guardrail; the agent refuses and escalates rather than
       inventing a limit; certifying-staff sign-off gate.
- Certification credibility
    -> decision-support framing, audit trail, and named standards (EASA ML
       learning assurance, SAE G-34/AS6983) — building for the audit from day one.
